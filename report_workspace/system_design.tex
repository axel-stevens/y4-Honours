\chapter{System Design}
\label{ch:system-design}

This section covers the overall design of the built system, the interfaces between subsystems, and the system requirements of the project. The purpose is to provide an overview of the design and the constraints, and demonstrate where the four objectives of the project are in the overall system. This section concludes with a short summary of the status of the system requirements after completing the project.  

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:sysdesign-architecture}

A diagram of the overall system architecture is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:system-design_system_diagram}. The system shows the key subsystems and their interactions, as well as their physical locations. The pilot's switch signal goes from the transmitter to the flight controller (via a receiver). This signal is further propagated to the \gls{sbc} via the MAVLink connection. Then the program on the \gls{sbc} launches the capture executable, which drives the \gls{sdr} over \gls{usb}~2.0. The same MAVLink connection carries \gls{gps}, position, attitude, battery, and flight mode. Both the capture signal and telemetry data sets, as one program stamps both the data streams with a single runtime clock and writes each capture filename into a dataset row. The \gls{sdr} and \gls{sbc} are both powered by a 22.2 V auxiliary power supply that is converted to 6 V for the \gls{sdr} and 5 V for the \gls{sbc}. The \gls{sdr}, \gls{sbc}, and power module are enclosed in a 3D-Printed compartment underneath the battery box from the drone, and the antenna is fixed on an external 45$^{\circ}$ mount.

\section{Requirements and Verification}
\label{sec:sysdesign-requirements}

The system requirements are derived from the specifications for technical objectives 1.1 to 1.6 in the project plan, and from its scope constraints. The requirements are regrouped in this section under the four objectives. Each requirement has an ID, a definition, a written account of its result, and the requirement's status. The status "Met" means the requirement was verified by test, inspection or demonstration. The status "Partly Met" the requirement was met in some partial capacity by test, demonstration or inspection. The status "Not Verified" means a test, demonstration, or inspection was not possible to verify the requirement and no analysis was conducted. The status "Not Met" means the requirement was confirmed to be not met by test, demonstration, or inspection.

Overall, thirteen requirements were met, five requirements were partly met, five were not verified, and one was not met. For the partly met requirements, R0.2 was a mass estimation, the drone was never weighed. R0.3 could only be partly met because only ground tests were conducted. For R3.4, the power is drawn from the drone's auxiliary supply, so it is technically not independent. For R4.5, the switch starts at a fixed caputure length, however the \gls{gps} logs continuously. R0.1, R2.2, and R3.2 require a flight test, so they are unverified. R1.4 is dependent on pending anachoic tests. R2.3 was not checked during testing, as the ground integration test was aimed at verifying base functionality, and so it was out of scope. R2.4 was not met, as the gimbal was descoped after jitter confirmed its infeasibility, and replaced by a 45$^{\circ}$ mount. The core functional chain from pilot trigger to radar capture synchronised with the drone telemetry log is met. The remaining gaps in the project are items dependent on flight tests, and items that are predicated on accuracy. The remaining gaps are characterised by all functional requirements not listed as "Met".
